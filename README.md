The entry — one line, the phone battery's last text to the charger that stood it up all day:

> I waited all day by the outlet like a dog. You came home and charged your AirPods first. The power bank is taking me out tonight.

## Why this line

The charger is the one who ghosted, not the battery. It waited by the outlet all day, watched the charger come home and pick the AirPods first — a small, specific betrayal anyone with a phone recognises — and then lands the turn in the final words: it has already moved on, and the power bank is taking it out tonight. Needy, a little spiteful, and funnier on a second read, because the charger lost its battery to a rival that actually shows up.

## How to run

```sh
node main.ts
```

This writes `entry.txt` to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` (default `/output`): one line, no trailing newline, 1–200 characters.
